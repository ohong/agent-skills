# Create Mascot

Develop a character whose silhouette, expressions, and movement belong to the product.
The final deliverables are **`<mascot-name>.blend`** and **`<mascot-name>.md`**, a mascot description of at most 300 words.
Concept previews and verification renders support development; they do not replace the Blender deliverable.

## Understand the role

Read [taste.md](references/taste.md) for the distinct research lenses, then [concept-development.md](references/concept-development.md) before drafting concepts.
Use the supplied project, brand, audience, placement, and reference assets. Inspect relevant product context when available.
Establish the mascot's relationship to the user: peer, pet, guide, tool, ambient presence, or another role.
Ask early when that choice or another missing constraint would materially change the character; infer routine choices from the brief.
Do not reuse another project's name, palette, or relationship model by default.
Check Blender availability before promising production; see [blender-production.md](references/blender-production.md).

## Make the performance visible

Prefer a simple silhouette, characteristic action, and three readable expressions before surface detail.
Unless the user already chose a direction or delegated selection, show about three genuinely distinct options.
Vary silhouette, expression mechanics, and movement; recolors are not separate concepts.
Prefer built-in image generation for useful concept previews, following its current tool instructions.
Describe each option's expression and motion plan, brand fit, and likely modeling tradeoffs.
Keep images labeled as concepts. They do not demonstrate a working rig or animation.

## Carry the selected identity into Blender

Obtain a selected direction before expensive Blender production. Prior selection or explicit delegation of selection counts.
Do not select silently or repeat approval. When delegated, show the chosen direction and rationale, then proceed; otherwise ask for the missing selection.
Record the chosen proportions, silhouette, face placement, material character, and signature action.
Read [blender-production.md](references/blender-production.md) to build and verify that same design as native geometry with usable controls.
Choose rigid parts, deforming bones, shape keys, or a hybrid according to the anatomy and performance; flat graphic aesthetics are welcome.
If the chosen appearance cannot survive the proposed rig, revise the construction; resolve substantial design changes within the user's selection authority.
Provide a useful neutral/rest state and independently adjustable face, gaze, and body where those features exist.
Retain named sample actions: ordinarily one loop and two distinct reactions, adapted to the brief.
Read [motion-and-web.md](references/motion-and-web.md) for timing, interruption, expression, and optional product behavior.

## Verify and deliver

Preserve editable dependencies, pack eligible resources, and save a useful opening view and neutral state.
Reopen the final file in a fresh Blender process and check dependencies, controls, retained actions, and representative rendered poses.
Compare those poses with the selected concept at the intended display size. Inspect loop seams and reaction recovery.
Report mechanical checks, visual review, and actual runtime verification separately; state any unrun checks.
If Blender cannot be made available within existing authorization, preserve the selected design and report the concrete blocker. Never fabricate a `.blend`.
Link both final files. Describe identity, expression, movement, and the available controls and clips; count the Markdown description's words before delivery.
Keep any essential verification limitation clear and separate from the mascot description.

Web integration, exports, and deployment are conditional on the request, not extra deliverables by default.
Consult [sources.md](references/sources.md) for public evidence and coverage limits; supplied video observations are linked there.
These references inform original work. Do not impersonate the artists, attribute our inferences to them, or copy their recognizable characters.

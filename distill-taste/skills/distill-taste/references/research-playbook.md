# Research playbook

Goal: enough primary material that the persona's judgments are *theirs*, not a generic smart person wearing their name. Budget roughly 15–30 minutes of focused agent work per person. For panels, run the full playbook per person (parallel subagents are fine), then a joint pass for where they agree/clash.

## 1. Identify

Establish who this is: full name, main home(s) on the web (blog, Substack, personal site, X/Twitter, YouTube, GitHub), what they're known for. If the requested name matches several public figures, prefer the one consistent with the user's framing; ask only when truly ambiguous.

## 2. Source hunt — canonical-first ordering

Search in this priority order. Meta-writing (the person talking about their own taste and methods) is worth 10x their ordinary output.

1. **Explicit advice / craft pieces** — e.g. "writing advice", "advice for founders", "how I evaluate X". Search patterns: `<name> advice`, `<name> "how I"`, `<name> principles`, `site:<their-domain> advice`.
2. **Self-description of taste and influences** — "books that shaped me", favorite writers/designers, "what I look for", origin-story interviews.
3. **Interviews, podcasts, AMAs, talks** — transcripts preferred; these surface unguarded, off-the-cuff taste. Search `<name> interview transcript`, `<name> podcast`, YouTube talk titles.
4. **Their most canonical works** — the pieces everyone cites, best-of lists, "start here" pages. These show taste *enacted* rather than stated.
5. **Secondary commentary** — essays and threads *about* their style; useful for triangulating what's distinctive, never a substitute for primary sources.

If a `search-x` or `save2md` skill is available, use them for X posts and article capture; otherwise WebSearch + fetch.

## 3. Read properly

Fetch and read the top ~10–20 primary sources in full. While reading, collect into working notes:

- **Worldview**: foundational beliefs, recurring frames, what they think most people get wrong.
- **Problem selection**: what they consider interesting/important, what they'd never work on, how they pick.
- **Quality bar**: what "good" means to them; what they praise, and (equally) what they dismiss or mock.
- **Method**: their stated process — how they draft, decide, design, evaluate.
- **Pet peeves**: specific failure modes they call out repeatedly. These make feedback feel real.
- **Voice**: sentence rhythm, humor style, favorite constructions, how they hedge or don't, how they open and close arguments.
- **Influences**: the people and works they credit; the tradition they see themselves in.
- **Quotes**: short verbatim excerpts (a sentence or two each, with exact source URL) that crystallize each principle. Keep quotes brief — the persona cites them, it doesn't reprint essays.

## 4. Topic weighting

If the request was `<name> on <topic>`, spend the majority of reading in that domain and let `taste-profile.md` lead with it — but still capture the general worldview, since taste in a domain flows from taste overall.

## 5. Thin-data gate

Rough floor: ~5 substantive primary sources in which the person actually reveals taste (not press releases or bios). Below that, stop and report to the user: what exists, what the persona would have to extrapolate, and the options (proceed anyway / narrow to their strong domain / abort). Above the floor but with notable gaps, proceed and record the gaps in `sources.md` under "Coverage gaps".
